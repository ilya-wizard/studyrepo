## Fresh themes investigated

### 1. Custom-contract invoice drafting for small B2B SaaS — REJECT generic wedge
**Pain:** Fresh Aug 2026 Reddit posts describe B2B SaaS teams using QBO + Stripe while manually drafting invoices because each customer has custom tiers, usage rules or discounts. A separate May 2026 founder reported 8–10 hours/month of manual usage export, pricing application, invoice generation and accounting prep before building an internal automation.

**Why it looked promising:** precise buyer, measurable recurring labor, direct existing stack, human-approval preference, easy <=€500 concierge test.

**Why it fails promotion:** LedgerUp now directly covers the exact workflow: reads signed contracts, extracts custom pricing/discounts/milestones/usage thresholds, drafts or creates invoices in Stripe/QuickBooks/NetSuite/Xero, supports usage/tiered/hybrid pricing, flags ambiguity, and routes one-click human approvals. Maxio, Zenskar/UsageBox-class billing systems and other contract-to-cash vendors further crowd the category. There is no surviving narrow wedge yet.

**Reopen if:** 3+ small B2B SaaS companies explicitly reject LedgerUp/Maxio/etc. for price, setup, supported pricing semantics or trust and will pay for a lighter manual pilot.

### 2. Provider-controlled credentialing / CAQH integrity monitoring — WATCH, below threshold
**Pain:** Fresh 2026 therapist/dietitian/provider discussions report credentialing vendors/employers entering incorrect CAQH data, requesting credentials, repeated attestations, multi-state payer fragmentation and practitioners spending time correcting mistakes. A merger thread describes payer-by-payer update chaos and risk of claim disruption.

**Potential second-order wedge:** provider-owned audit/control layer that tracks what changed in credentialing records, delegate access, upcoming re-attestations and payer/entity mismatches — rather than another generic credentialing service.

**Why not promoted:** willingness to pay for the integrity/audit layer is unproven; many credentialing vendors already offer maintenance/tracking; CAQH/DataSpring access constraints may prevent reliable change monitoring; individual clinicians are price-sensitive. Estimated score ~71–74 depending on buyer.

**Reopen if:** independent providers or small group practices show repeated paid demand specifically for credentialing-data control/audit after bad experiences with Headway/Grow/Nourish/credentialing vendors.

### 3. Small independent insurance agency operating system — REJECT generic wedge
**Pain:** 2026 agency owners complain Applied Epic/EZLynx are clunky/expensive, important information is spread across AMS + CRM + paper, and tasks depend on staff memory. Small agencies seek inexpensive, modern systems.

**Why it fails promotion:** the market is rapidly filling with direct modern small-agency alternatives (OneagencyOS, HarborIQ, simpleAMS, InsureWise and others) priced specifically for 1–5 person agencies. Generic lightweight AMS is no longer an attractive wedge.

**Possible second-order area investigated:** acquisition/book cleanup and legacy-data migration. This is also directly covered by RecordLinker, Epic Optimizer, Applied tools and specialized service providers.

**Reopen if:** a specific workflow around an incumbent AMS has repeated evidence that current migration/automation vendors cannot support it.
