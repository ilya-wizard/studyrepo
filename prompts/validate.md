# Deep Validation Protocol

Only run this protocol for candidates that survive initial scoring.

## 1. Problem reality
Find at least 5 independent evidence points when possible. Distinguish frequency from intensity. Identify whether the problem is chronic, episodic, or triggered by a specific event.

## 2. Existing spend
Look for money already being spent on software, services, contractors, consultants, ads, hardware, wasted labor, fees, or avoidable losses. Existing spend is stronger than stated willingness to pay.

## 3. Alternatives
Map direct products, indirect substitutes, manual workflows, and "do nothing". Record pricing, audience, strengths, and repeated complaints.

## 4. Wedge
Define the narrowest user/problem combination where a new entrant could be meaningfully better. "Uses AI" is not a wedge by itself.

## 5. Distribution
Name the first 20-100 potential users and where they can be reached. Prefer concentrated communities, searchable intent, integrations/ecosystems, partnerships, or outbound lists over broad consumer advertising.

## 6. €500 validation experiment
Design the cheapest credible test before building the full product. Examples: concierge service, landing page + direct outreach, paid pilot, manual prototype, browser extension, spreadsheet tool, fake-door test where ethically appropriate, or a narrow hardware proof-of-concept.

## 7. Failure test
Actively search for reasons the opportunity is bad: failed startups, low retention, regulation, incumbent bundling, poor margins, impossible distribution, behavior-change burden, or a market that complains but does not pay.

## 8. Decision
Return one of:
- PRIORITY — strong evidence and worth testing now
- VALIDATE — promising but one key uncertainty remains
- WATCH — real problem, weak timing/distribution/business model
- REJECT — evidence does not support investment

The report must state what evidence would change the decision.
